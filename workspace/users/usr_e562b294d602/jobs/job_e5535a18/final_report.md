**Report on Zakir Naik**

---

### 1. Biography  
- **Full Name:** Zakir Abdul Karim Naik  
- **Date of Birth:** 18 October 1965  
- **Place of Birth:** Bombay (now Mumbai), Maharashtra, India  
- **Education:** Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) from the University of Mumbai  
- **Verification:** Biographical details (birth date, place, MBBS) are **substantiated** by the Tavily result and corroborated by multiple sources (e.g., Wikipedia, All American Speakers).  

### 2. Career & Organizations  
- **Early Profession:** Worked as a medical physician before transitioning to public speaking and dawah (Islamic outreach).  
- **Islamic Research Foundation (IRF):** Founded in 1991; a non‑profit organization that promotes Islamic understanding through television, radio, internet, and publications.  
- **Peace TV Network:** Founder and president; launched after IRF, described as the largest religious satellite‑channel network.  
- **Verification:** The founding of IRF (1991) and Peace TV, as well as Naik’s role as founder/president, are **substantiated** by IRF descriptions, All American Speakers, and Wikipedia. His prior medical career is also **substantiated** (All American Speakers).  

### 3. Influence & Reach  
- **Peace TV Viewership (June 2016):** English broadcast in HD/SD on 15 satellites worldwide, reaching over **100 million viewers**; the total Peace TV network viewership exceeded **200 million**.  
- **Audience Composition:** Claims that roughly **25 %** of viewers are non‑Muslims.  
- **Awards:** Received the **King Faisal International Prize for Service to Islam** in 2015 (presented by King Salman bin Abdul Aziz Al Saud on 1 March 2015).  
- **Public Image:** Recognized as a non‑Arabic Salafi scholar, public orator, and authority on comparative religion; known for delivering speeches in English while wearing a suit and tie rather than traditional attire.  
- **Verification:** Viewership figures (100 M+ English, 200 M+ total) and the 2015 King Faisal Prize are **substantiated** by zakirnaik.com, Peace TV intro page, and the award page. The claim about 25 % non‑Muslim viewership appears on the same Peace TV page but lacks independent verification; it is therefore **unverified**. The description of his speaking style and public image is **substantiated** by Wikipedia and All American Speakers.  

### 4. Controversies & Legal Issues (India)  
- **2016 Accusations:** While abroad, the BJP‑led Indian government accused Naik of “propagating hate speech” and money laundering; consequently, the government banned IRF and Peace TV broadcasts in India.  
- **Legal Response:** Naik denied the charges, did not return to India, and obtained permanent residency in Malaysia.  
- **Interpol Notices:** India’s National Investigation Agency (NIA) sought an Interpol red notice for his arrest; the request was refused three times due to insufficient evidence.  
- **Broadcast Bans:** Peace TV channels have been banned in India, Bangladesh, Canada, Sri Lanka, and the UK under hate‑speech laws (the mobile app remains accessible in some jurisdictions).  
- **Verification:** The 2016 accusations, bans on IRF/Peace TV, Naik’s denial, his residence in Malaysia, and the Interpol red‑notice refusals are **substantiated** by BBC articles, Wikipedia, and official government statements. The specific allegation of money laundering remains **on record but not convicted**, and the lack of sufficient evidence for terror‑related charges is noted in the verification summary.  

### 5. Notable Statements  
- **1996 Remark on Terrorism:** Naik stated, “Every Muslim should be a terrorist. The thing is that if he [Osama] is terrorizing the terrorist, then he is following Islam.” He also called America “the biggest terrorist.”  
- **Contextual Clarification (2010):** Naik later claimed the quote was taken out of context, explaining that “terrorist” means one who terrorises anti‑social elements.  
- **Other Provocative Statements:** Listed among six provocative remarks in a 2015 Firstpost article titled “‘Every Muslim should be a terrorist’: Zakir Naik and six of his most provocative statements.”  
- **Verification:** The existence of the 1996 video clip and the 2010 contextual explanation are **substantiated** by Wikipedia and the Firstpost article. However, the precise meaning/intent of the statement remains **disputed**, with supporters emphasizing the contextual explanation and critics viewing it as hate‑speech. The verification summary notes that claims of decontextualization exist on both sides, rendering the interpretation **contested**.  

### 6. Alleged Influence on Terrorists  
- **BBC Reports:** Detained al‑Qaeda activists told authorities that Naik’s sermons influenced them.  
- **Firstpost (2015):** Noted that two terrorists killed in the 2016 Dhaka attack were found to have been influenced by Naik’s sermons.  
- **Verification:** These allegations are **on record** but have not resulted in convictions; the verification summary classifies them as **unverified** or **insufficiently evidenced**, noting that Interpol red‑notice refusals reflect a lack of proof for terror‑related charges.  

### 7. Current Status  
- **Legal Standing:** Considered a wanted fugitive by Indian authorities; resides in Malaysia as a permanent resident.  
- **Activity:** Continues to deliver lectures and appears on Peace TV (where accessible) and online platforms.  
- **Verification:** His status as a fugitive sought by India and his Malaysian residency are **substantiated** by Wikipedia and BBC reports. Ongoing public activity is observable but not independently verified in the raw findings.
